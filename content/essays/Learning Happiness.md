For a long time, I thought happiness was something you arrived at.

After the right job. After making enough money. After becoming better at what I do. After travelling more, achieving more, fixing a few things about myself. 

There is always next thing in the line that needs to be checked.

It took me a while to notice how unreliable that formula was.

There was always something between me and happiness.

I suspect many of us live this way without noticing. We treat the present as a waiting room for the life we actually want.

But the future is an excellent place to postpone your life.

Working with animals has taught me something about attention. You learn to notice small things: a change in appetite, a different way of standing, a subtle change in behaviour. Often, what matters is already there. You simply have to notice it.

Maybe happiness works in much the same way.

A good conversation. A quiet morning. A healthy body. Someone you love sitting beside you. Work that is difficult but meaningful. The absurdity of something that makes you laugh.

None of these things are particularly spectacular. Yet they are life.

The mind is remarkably good at making the familiar invisible. We get used to what we once desperately wanted, then start waiting for something else.

So perhaps learning happiness is partly learning to notice.

But it is also learning to stop waiting.

There will always be another problem to solve, another goal to reach, another version of yourself to become. If you make happiness conditional on everything being in order, you may spend your entire life preparing to live.

This doesn't mean abandoning ambition. Build things. Improve yourself. Want more.

Just don't make your future self responsible for enjoying your present life.

You don't need permission for today to count.

At some point, you realize that happiness was not hiding somewhere ahead of you.

You were simply standing still, waiting for it to arrive.

And then you start living.

We spend years learning how to make a living, how to become competent, how to solve problems.

Maybe we should spend some time learning how to be alive, too.
