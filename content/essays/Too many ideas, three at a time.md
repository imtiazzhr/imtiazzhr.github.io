My problem has never been a shortage of ideas.

At any given moment I have a full-time clinical job and, next to it, a list that looks something like this: a vet company, a software product, a hospital I'd like to open one day, a business back home in Pakistan, a trading habit, a stack of courses, fifty books I mean to read and fifty countries I mean to visit. Every one of those feels important, and every one of them feels urgent on the day I think about it.

For years the result was the same. Lots of starting, very little finishing, and a quiet feeling at the end of each month that I'd been busy without getting anywhere.

## I tried to fix it with tools

My first instinct was that I needed a better system, so I kept switching apps. Notion, Roam Research, Logseq, back to Notion, then Obsidian. Each one felt like a fresh start. Each one slowly turned into a junk drawer full of half-finished plans.

It took me too long to see that the tool wasn't the problem. I was using each new app to hold *more* goals, when what I needed was a way to hold *fewer*.

## A year is too long, a week is too short

Yearly goals didn't work for me. In January everything seems possible, so I'd write down ten goals. By March the list was stale, and by June I'd stopped looking at it. A year is long enough to always feel like there's time.

Weekly plans had the opposite problem. A week is too short for anything meaningful to move, so every week felt like a failure or a scramble.

Twelve weeks sits in the middle. It's long enough to finish something real and short enough that the deadline is always in view. By week three you can already feel the end coming.

## The rule that actually matters: three

The idea of the [[The 12 Week Year|twelve-week year]] isn't mine; plenty of people have written about it. The part that changed things for me is the limit I added on top: **no more than three goals in a cycle.**

Three is small enough to hold in my head. It forces me to choose, and choosing is the part I've always avoided. When a new idea turns up mid-cycle, and it always does, it doesn't get deleted. It gets *parked*: written down, dated, and left alone until the next cycle begins. Parking works because it's not a "no", only a "not now", and I can say "not now" to almost anything.

The same rule of three runs all the way down. Three focus tasks a week. Each day, one block of deep work, three short tasks and three bits of maintenance. When everything is limited to three, it becomes obvious what doesn't fit.

## Honest status

I'm writing this at the start of a new cycle, not the end, so treat it as a promise rather than a result. My three goals this time are building my vet software, growing personally (reading, and learning to write in public) and doing good clinical work. Everything else is parked, including some ideas I'm very excited about.

I'll write the review at the end of the cycle and tell you how it went.
