*or The Modern Prometheus*

**Mary Shelley** · 1818 · Classic novel
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Frankenstein is framed by the letters of Robert Walton, an explorer sailing towards the North Pole, whose crew rescues an exhausted stranger named Victor Frankenstein from the ice. Victor explains how, as a gifted young student, he became obsessed with discovering the secret of life and succeeded in bringing a creature assembled from dead matter to life. Horrified by what he has made, he abandons it.

The creature, intelligent and sensitive but rejected by everyone who sees him, teaches himself to speak and read and grows bitter in his isolation. He then confronts his maker with demands of his own. Mary Shelley, who began the novel at eighteen, uses the story to examine ambition, scientific responsibility, loneliness and whether cruelty is born or made.

## Key insights

- Creators are responsible for what they make. Victor's failure lies less in the act of creation than in abandoning the being he brought into the world.
- Unchecked ambition narrows vision. Victor works in secret, neglecting his health, his family and the ethical questions his project raises.
- Rejection shapes behaviour. The creature begins wanting kindness and connection, and the novel asks how far his later actions stem from how he is treated.
- Isolation damages judgement. Victor's secrecy and the creature's exclusion both show how loneliness can distort thinking and fuel destructive choices.
- The novel works as an early warning about technology: knowledge gained quickly, without reflection or oversight, can outrun our ability to manage its consequences.

*Read it if you are interested in the ethics of creation and technology, or want the original story behind the familiar myth.*
