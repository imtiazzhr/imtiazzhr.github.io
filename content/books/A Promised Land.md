**Barack Obama** · 2020 · Memoir
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

A Promised Land is the first volume of Barack Obama's presidential memoir. It follows his path from a restless young man searching for purpose to community organiser, Illinois state senator, US senator and, in 2008, the first Black president of the United States. Most of the book covers his first term and ends with the 2011 raid that killed Osama bin Laden.

Obama writes candidly about how hard decisions get made: rescuing the economy after the financial crash, passing the Affordable Care Act, responding to the Deepwater Horizon oil spill, and weighing war and diplomacy abroad. He is open about his doubts, his compromises and the strain the job placed on his family. Throughout, he reflects on democracy, race and the deepening divisions in American politics.

## Key insights

- Hard problems rarely have clean solutions. Issues that reach a leader's desk usually involve choosing between bad and worse, so judge decisions against realistic alternatives.
- Invite disagreement before you decide. Obama made a habit of seeking dissenting views in meetings, including from quieter and more junior people in the room.
- Progress in a democracy is usually incremental. A flawed law that helps millions can be worth more than holding out for a perfect one.
- Good policy is not enough on its own. A sound economic rescue can still be politically punishing if people feel it rewarded the powerful and ignored them.
- Public life has private costs. Obama is frank about the strain his ambitions placed on Michelle and their daughters, a reminder to weigh those costs deliberately.

*Read it if you want an inside view of how a president weighs hard choices, told with unusual candour and reflection.*
