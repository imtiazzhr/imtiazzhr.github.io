**Robert Henri** · 1923 · Art
<span class="book-shelf">Currently reading</span>

The Art Spirit gathers the teaching notes, letters and talks of Robert Henri, an American painter and influential teacher of the early twentieth century. The material was compiled by his former student Margery Ryerson and reads as a series of short reflections rather than a structured manual.

Henri covers practical matters such as colour, composition, portraiture and working from life, but his main concern is attitude. He urges students to paint with conviction, to express what moves them rather than follow academic formulas, and to treat art as a way of seeing and living. The point, he suggests, is less the finished picture than the alert, engaged state of mind that produces it. Many readers outside painting use it as a guide to creative work.

## Key insights

- Focus on the state of mind, not only the product. When you are fully engaged and attentive, good work tends to follow as a by-product.
- Work quickly and decisively when capturing an impression. Hesitation and overworking can drain the life from a sketch, a piece of writing or an idea.
- Study the masters to understand their principles, not to copy their surface style. Borrowed technique without personal conviction tends to produce lifeless work.
- Start from what genuinely interests you. Henri believed the artist's own feeling about a subject matters more than fashionable themes or approved methods.
- The artistic spirit is not limited to artists. Anyone who does their work with care, curiosity and imagination, in any field, can work in that spirit.

*Read it if you do any kind of creative work and want short, encouraging notes on seeing clearly and working with conviction.*
