This is a school textbook by Huma Naz Sethi, written for the Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies course, specifically the paper on the geography and environment of Pakistan. It surveys the country's physical landscape, from its mountain ranges to the plains of the Indus, along with its climate, water resources and natural resources such as forests, minerals and energy. It also covers agriculture, industry, transport, population and environmental problems.

The book is organised around exam preparation, using maps, diagrams, graphs and tables, with practice questions in the style of Cambridge papers. Published by Peak Publishing, it has been revised through several editions as the syllabus has changed. For general readers, it offers a structured overview of how land, water and climate shape Pakistan's economy.

## Key insights

- Much of Pakistan's farming depends on the Indus river system, so irrigation, dams and water storage are central to understanding its economy.
- Climate shapes livelihoods; monsoon rains, extreme temperatures and dry regions influence which crops grow, where people live and which hazards they face.
- Development can damage the land it depends on; problems such as deforestation, waterlogging and salinity show the environmental costs of growth.
- Geography helps explain economics; the location of industries, power stations and transport routes reflects raw materials, markets and water supply.
- The course rewards skills as well as facts; interpreting maps, graphs and tables is a core part of what the book trains students to do.

*Read it if you are studying O Level Pakistan Studies or want a structured introduction to the geography of Pakistan.*
