<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

When We Believed in Mermaids is a family drama about two sisters and a secret. Kit, an emergency room doctor in Santa Cruz, has spent fifteen years grieving her sister Josie, who was believed killed in a terrorist attack on a train. Then Kit glimpses a woman who looks like Josie in news footage of a nightclub fire in Auckland, and she travels to New Zealand to find the truth.

Told from both sisters' viewpoints, the novel moves between the present and a childhood by the beach, shaped by a difficult home life and a troubled teenage boy who entered their lives. Barbara O'Neal explores why someone might abandon their identity, how early trauma echoes into adulthood, and whether honesty and forgiveness can repair a broken bond.

## Key insights

- Unresolved childhood trauma rarely stays in the past. It can shape adult choices, relationships and even the urge to disappear and start again.
- Siblings can remember the same childhood very differently. Hearing the other person's version is often where understanding and repair begin.
- Running from pain can bring temporary relief, but unfinished business tends to follow you until it is faced directly.
- Secrets kept to protect others can do as much damage as the truths they hide, especially within families.
- Forgiveness is a process rather than a single moment. It often starts with understanding why someone made their choices, without excusing the harm.

*Read it if you enjoy emotional family dramas about sisters, secrets and second chances, with vivid coastal settings.*
