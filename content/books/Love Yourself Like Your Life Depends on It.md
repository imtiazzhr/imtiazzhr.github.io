<span class="book-shelf">Currently reading</span>

Kamal Ravikant wrote this short book after a period of illness, grief and professional failure left him at a low point. He made a private vow to love himself and describes the simple practices that helped him recover.

His main tool is an inner mantra, repeated silently throughout the day until it becomes the mind's default thought. He supports it with brief daily meditation and a simple visualisation. His argument is that the mind follows well-worn grooves, so deliberately repeating a kind thought can gradually replace habits of self-criticism. The book is personal rather than scientific, written in short, informal chapters, and can be read in a single sitting. It is as much a record of one man's recovery as a guide.

## Key insights

- Thoughts wear grooves in the mind. Repeating a chosen phrase, even before you believe it, can gradually displace automatic self-criticism.
- Treat self-kindness as a daily discipline, like exercise, rather than a mood to wait for. Consistency matters more than intensity.
- When facing a choice, ask whether someone who truly loved themselves would accept this situation. The question clarifies boundaries with people, habits and work.
- Reinforce the new thought when the mind is quiet. Short meditation sessions built around the mantra help it take root.
- Ravikant suggests that as your inner voice becomes kinder, relationships and opportunities tend to improve, because people respond to how you carry yourself.

*Read it if you are hard on yourself and want a short, simple practice for changing your inner voice.*
