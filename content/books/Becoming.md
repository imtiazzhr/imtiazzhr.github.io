**Michelle Obama** · 2018 · Memoir
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Michelle Obama's memoir is divided into three parts, tracing her own development, her partnership with Barack Obama and her public life. She grew up in a small upstairs flat on Chicago's South Side in a close-knit family, while her father kept working for years despite multiple sclerosis. She went on to Princeton and Harvard Law School, then joined a corporate law firm, where she was asked to mentor a summer associate named Barack Obama.

She describes leaving law for public service, the strain of politics on family life, struggles with fertility, and raising two daughters in the public eye. The final section covers her eight years as First Lady. Throughout, she reflects candidly on race, class, identity and self-doubt.

## Key insights

- Growth is a process, not a finish line; she treats becoming as continuing work rather than arriving at a final version of yourself.
- Other people's low expectations are not a verdict; she applied to Princeton despite a school counsellor doubting she was suited to it.
- Self-doubt can be answered with evidence; she describes proving to herself, one setting after another, that she belonged there.
- A successful career can still be the wrong one; her move away from corporate law began by asking what work would feel meaningful.
- Marriage takes deliberate work; she describes couples counselling and realising that her happiness was her own responsibility.

*Read it if you want a candid account of ambition, family and public life from a First Lady who was wary of politics.*
