**Charles William Eliot** · 1909 · Anthology
<span class="book-shelf">Currently reading</span>

The Harvard Classics is a 51-volume anthology of classic writing chosen by Charles W. Eliot, who served as president of Harvard University for four decades. Eliot had claimed that a five-foot shelf of books, read for fifteen minutes a day, could give anyone the essentials of a liberal education. The publisher P. F. Collier and Son challenged him to make the selection, and the first volumes appeared in 1909. Complete editions often add the twenty-volume Shelf of Fiction, compiled later.

The collection spans philosophy, history, science, religion, poetry, drama and travel. It includes Plato, Marcus Aurelius, Homer, Dante, Shakespeare, Adam Smith, Darwin and Benjamin Franklin's autobiography, alongside sacred texts from several traditions. It is a library to dip into over years, not a book to read straight through.

## Key insights

- Small, steady reading adds up. Fifteen minutes a day over several years can cover a wide range of ideas, which is Eliot's central promise.
- Go to primary sources. Reading Darwin, Smith or Plato directly shows what they actually argued, not what summaries say they argued.
- Use the reading guide and index to follow a theme across volumes rather than ploughing through the set in order.
- Any canon reflects its editor's era. This one is largely Western and male, so pair it with voices and traditions it leaves out.
- Reading across fields, from science to poetry to history, helps you spot connections that specialists in one discipline can miss.

*Read it if you want a structured, lifelong reading programme drawn from the Western canon to dip into a little each day.*
