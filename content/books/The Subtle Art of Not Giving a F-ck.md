*A Counterintuitive Approach to Living a Good Life*

**Mark Manson** · 2016 · Self-help
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Mark Manson argues that much modern self-help, with its constant positivity and promise that everyone is special, leaves people less happy. The problem is not caring too little but caring about too many things, often the wrong ones. Since life always involves pain and problems, the real question is which problems are worth having and which values are worth struggling for.

In blunt, profane language, and with stories ranging from a guitarist fired from Metallica to a Japanese soldier who kept fighting in the jungle for decades after the Second World War, Manson makes the case for responsibility, honesty, doubt and commitment. He urges readers to accept their limits, including mortality, and to choose carefully what deserves their energy.

## Key insights

- You cannot avoid caring; you can only choose what to care about. Spend your limited attention on a few things that genuinely matter.
- Happiness comes from solving problems, not from having none. Choose goals whose struggles you can enjoy, not just whose rewards you want.
- Separate fault from responsibility. You may not be to blame for what happened, but you are responsible for how you respond.
- Motivation often follows action. When stuck, do something small; the action produces inspiration, which then produces more motivation.
- Prefer values that are grounded in reality, good for others and within your control over ones like pleasure, status or always being right.

*Read it if you are tired of relentless positivity and want blunt, practical advice about choosing what really matters.*
