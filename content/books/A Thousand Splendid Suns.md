**Khaled Hosseini** · 2007 · Novel
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Khaled Hosseini's second novel follows two Afghan women whose lives are joined by war and by marriage to the same man. Mariam, born out of wedlock to a wealthy businessman in Herat, grows up on the margins and is married off at fifteen to Rasheed, a much older shoemaker in Kabul. A generation later, Laila grows up in Kabul with a bookish father who wants his daughter educated. When the civil war of the 1990s destroys her family, she too ends up in Rasheed's house.

The story moves through decades of upheaval, including the Soviet war, the civil war and Taliban rule. Hosseini shows how public politics reach into private homes, and how two women who begin as rivals come to depend on each other.

## Key insights

- Rivals can become allies once they see their shared situation clearly; Mariam and Laila's bond grows from resentment into deep loyalty.
- Politics is felt most sharply at home; each change of regime alters what the women can wear, study, earn and do.
- Quiet endurance is not the same as surrender; when open defiance is dangerous, small acts of care and survival become a form of resistance.
- How a child is valued shapes her for life; Mariam's early shame as an unwanted daughter colours her expectations for decades.
- Rights such as schooling and free movement can vanish quickly; the novel shows how fast a society can shut half its people out.

*Read it if you want a moving, character-driven novel about women's friendship and endurance through decades of Afghan conflict.*
