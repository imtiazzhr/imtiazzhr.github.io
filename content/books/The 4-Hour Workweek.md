**Timothy Ferriss** · 2007 · Productivity
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Tim Ferriss challenges the habit of working for decades and deferring enjoyment until retirement. He describes the New Rich, people who value time and mobility more than money, and sets out a four-step process called DEAL: Definition, Elimination, Automation and Liberation. Definition means setting specific goals and confronting fears. Elimination uses the 80/20 principle and Parkinson's Law to cut low-value work and information. Automation means outsourcing tasks and building a small business, which he calls a muse, that earns income without constant attention. Liberation covers negotiating remote work and taking mini-retirements.

Some tactics now feel dated, and the four-hour headline is more provocation than promise. But its central questions, about what you actually want and why you are postponing it, remain useful.

## Key insights

- Try fear-setting: write down the worst realistic outcome of a change, how you would repair it, and the cost of doing nothing.
- Combine the 80/20 rule with Parkinson's Law: identify the few tasks that matter most and give them short, firm deadlines.
- Adopt a low-information diet. Ignore news and inputs you cannot act on, and batch email instead of checking it constantly.
- Before building a product, test demand cheaply with a simple advert or landing page and see whether people will actually pay.
- Dreamlining turns vague wishes into specific goals with a monthly cost; the life you want is often cheaper than you assume.

*Read it if you want provocative ideas for escaping the default career path, cutting busywork and designing a more flexible life.*
