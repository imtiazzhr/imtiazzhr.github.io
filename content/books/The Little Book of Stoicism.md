*Timeless Wisdom to Gain Resilience, Confidence, and Calmness*

**Jonas Salzgeber** · 2019 · Philosophy
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Jonas Salzgeber offers a short, informal introduction to Stoicism for modern readers. The first part explains the philosophy: its origins in ancient Athens, well-known Stoics such as Zeno, Seneca, Epictetus and Marcus Aurelius, and its goal of eudaimonia, a flourishing life. He condenses the core into what he calls the Stoic Happiness Triangle: live with arete by expressing your best self in each moment, focus on what you can control, and take responsibility for how you respond. He also explains the Stoic view that destructive emotions come from our judgements rather than from events themselves.

The second, longer part sets out 55 practices, grouped around building resilience, handling hard situations and dealing with other people.

## Key insights

- Sort every worry into what you control, such as your judgements and actions, and what you do not; spend effort only on the first.
- Events do not upset us directly; our judgements about them do. Pausing before reacting creates room to choose a better response.
- Imagine setbacks in advance, including losing things you value. This softens shocks when they come and increases gratitude for what you have now.
- Add a reserve clause to plans: commit fully to acting, but accept that the outcome may differ, so frustration does not take over.
- When dealing with difficult people, remember your own faults and assume others act on their beliefs; this makes anger harder to sustain.

*Read it if you want a friendly, practical starting point for Stoicism with exercises you can use in daily life.*
