<span class="book-shelf">Currently reading</span>

The Bertrand Russell Collection is a digital compilation rather than a single book. The edition under this title gathers eight works published between 1912 and 1922, a period when Russell combined technical philosophy with increasingly public writing on politics and society. It includes The Problems of Philosophy, a short introduction to questions about knowledge and reality, and Mysticism and Logic, a set of essays on science, mathematics and the search for meaning.

Other works in the set examine the nature of the mind, the principles of a good political order and his critical verdict on Soviet communism after visiting Russia in 1920. Across them, Russell argues for clear reasoning, scepticism towards dogma, freedom of thought and institutions that encourage creativity rather than possessiveness. Contents vary between editions.

## Key insights

- Philosophy's value lies partly in its uncertainty. Asking questions without firm answers widens your thinking and loosens the grip of habit and prejudice.
- Past regularity does not guarantee the future. Russell's chicken, fed every day until the day it is killed, is a lasting warning about naive induction.
- Separate knowledge by acquaintance from knowledge by description. Much of what we claim to know comes second-hand, through descriptions and inference rather than direct experience.
- Good institutions nurture creative impulses, such as art, science and care for others, over possessive impulses aimed at acquiring and holding on to things.
- Admire an ideal without excusing its methods. Russell sympathised with communist aims but warned that dogma and concentrated power in Soviet Russia would betray them.

*Read it if you want a single volume sampling Russell's clear, sceptical thinking on knowledge, mind and politics.*
