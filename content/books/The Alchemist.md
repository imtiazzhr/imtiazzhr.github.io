**Paulo Coelho** · 1988 · Novel
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Santiago, a young shepherd in Andalusia, keeps dreaming of treasure hidden near the Egyptian pyramids. Encouraged by a mysterious old king who tells him about his Personal Legend, the thing he is meant to accomplish, he sells his flock and sets off for North Africa. Along the way he is robbed, works for a crystal merchant in Tangier, crosses the Sahara with a caravan, falls in love at an oasis and meets an alchemist who becomes his teacher.

Written as a simple fable, the novel explores following your dreams, reading the signs life offers, listening to your heart and overcoming the fear that stops people from trying. First published in Portuguese in 1988, it is one of the most translated books by a living author.

## Key insights

- Pursuing what you most want means leaving comfort and accepting risk; the story suggests that unlived dreams bring more regret than failure.
- Some people keep a dream unfulfilled to protect it from disappointment, as the crystal merchant does; notice if you are doing the same.
- Learning comes through experience as well as books; the contrast between Santiago and the Englishman shows the value of observing and doing.
- Anticipating pain can hurt more than the pain itself, so naming your fears honestly can loosen their hold on your decisions.
- The journey shapes the traveller. The people met and skills learned along the way are part of the reward, not just the destination.

*Read it if you want a short, simple fable about pursuing your dreams and paying attention to the journey.*
