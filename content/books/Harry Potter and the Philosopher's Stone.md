<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone introduces Harry, an orphan raised by an aunt and uncle who treat him as a burden. On his eleventh birthday he learns that he is a wizard, that his parents were killed by the dark wizard Voldemort, and that he is famous in a hidden magical world for surviving the attack as a baby.

Harry starts at Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry, where he makes friends with Ron Weasley and Hermione Granger, discovers a talent for Quidditch and faces hostility from a rival pupil and a harsh teacher. The friends begin to suspect that someone is trying to steal a valuable object hidden in the school. The novel explores friendship, courage, belonging and the difference between fame and character.

## Key insights

- Courage takes many forms. Standing up to your own friends when they are wrong can take as much bravery as facing an enemy.
- Dwelling on what we long for can stop us from living. The Mirror of Erised shows how a wish can quietly become a trap.
- Friendship is often forged through shared trouble. Harry, Ron and Hermione become close only after facing danger together and learning to value each other's strengths.
- Fame is not the same as character. Harry is celebrated for something he cannot remember and has to earn respect through his own choices.
- Belonging can be found as well as inherited. Harry discovers at Hogwarts the sense of home and acceptance his own relatives never gave him.

*Read it if you want an inviting fantasy about friendship and courage, or want to see why the series won over a generation.*
