**R.J. Palacio** · 2012 · Children's novel
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

August Pullman, known as Auggie, is a ten-year-old New Yorker born with a severe facial difference. After years of surgeries and home-schooling, he starts fifth grade at a private school, where other children stare, whisper and sometimes bully him. The novel follows his first year there as he makes friends, faces cruelty and tries to be treated like any other kid.

Palacio tells the story through several narrators, including Auggie, his older sister Via and classmates such as Jack and Summer. Their perspectives show how one child's difference affects his family, his friends and a whole school community, and how each of them carries struggles of their own. The book explores kindness, courage, loyalty and how we choose to treat people who look different.

## Key insights

- When you can choose between being right and being kind, kindness often matters more. Small acts of decency can change a child's whole year.
- Siblings of children with high needs can feel invisible. Via's chapters show that love and resentment can exist side by side.
- Hearing several narrators shows that everyone carries private struggles; behaviour that looks cold may come from fear, pressure or pain.
- Peer pressure can make decent children say cruel things. Real repair takes honest apology and changed behaviour, not only regret.
- Adults set the culture. Teachers, parents and school leaders decide whether cruelty is tolerated or challenged, and children notice which it is.

*Read it if you want a warm, accessible novel about kindness and difference that works for both children and adults.*
