**Joseph Heller** · 1961 · Classic novel
<span class="book-shelf">Want to read</span>

Catch-22 is a satirical novel set on a small island off the Italian coast during the Second World War. Captain John Yossarian, a bombardier in an American air squadron, has one overriding aim: to stay alive. His superiors keep raising the number of combat missions the men must fly before they can go home, and his attempts to escape run into circular military logic.

The title names the central trap. Any airman who asks to be grounded for insanity proves, by wanting to avoid danger, that he is sane, so he must keep flying. Told out of chronological order through a large cast of eccentric characters, the novel mixes farce with horror to attack bureaucracy, profiteering and the absurdity of war.

## Key insights

- Watch for circular rules. A catch-22 is a trap whose only exit is blocked by its own conditions, and bureaucracies create them surprisingly often.
- Institutions can pursue their own goals at the expense of the people inside them. Yossarian's commanders care more about promotion and appearances than their men's lives.
- Profit can override loyalty. Milo Minderbinder's trading empire shows how market logic, left unchecked, can justify almost anything.
- Sanity depends on the system. In an insane environment, the person who refuses to accept its logic may be the most reasonable one.
- Humour can make horror visible. Heller uses farce and repetition to show how the absurd and the tragic sit side by side in war.

*Read it if you enjoy dark, absurd humour and want a sharp satire of bureaucracy and war that still feels relevant.*
