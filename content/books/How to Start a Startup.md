*The Silicon Valley Playbook for Entrepreneurs*

**Tarun Agarwal** · 2016 · Business
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

This independently produced guide condenses lessons from How to Start a Startup, a Stanford University course taught by the Silicon Valley accelerator Y Combinator. It draws on more than 25 Silicon Valley insiders, including Paul Graham, Reid Hoffman, Dustin Moskovitz, Peter Thiel, Marc Andreessen, Ben Horowitz and Pinterest's Ben Silbermann. Net proceeds from the book go to charitable causes.

The material follows the practical challenges of an early-stage company: raising money, building a product users love, hiring a strong team, getting press coverage, attracting customers and growing the business. It is organised as a playbook rather than a narrative, collecting advice that founders can test against their own situation. Readers who know the original lectures, which are freely available online, will find familiar ideas in condensed form.

## Key insights

- Build something a small group of users loves before chasing scale; many users who merely like a product rarely make it grow.
- Talk to users constantly and watch what they do, not only what they say, to decide what to build next.
- Early on, do things that do not scale, such as recruiting users by hand, to learn quickly and win your first customers.
- Hire slowly and carefully at the start; the first employees shape the culture and are hard to replace.
- Treat fundraising as a means, not a goal. Raise money when you can show progress, then return your focus to the business.

*Read it if you want a condensed overview of Y Combinator-style startup advice without watching the full lecture series.*
