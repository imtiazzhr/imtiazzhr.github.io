**Matthew McConaughey** · 2020 · Memoir
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Greenlights is Matthew McConaughey's memoir, assembled from decades of diaries, notes and poems. He tells stories from his Texas upbringing with tough, loving parents who had a stormy marriage, a formative exchange year in Australia, his switch from law to film school, and his rise from a small part in Dazed and Confused to Hollywood stardom.

The organising idea is the greenlight, a signal to go ahead. McConaughey argues that many red and yellow lights, such as setbacks, refusals and detours, turn out to be greenlights later if we learn from them. The book mixes humour, road trips and short maxims with a serious theme: living with intention. A key example is his decision to step away from romantic comedies and wait for more demanding roles.

## Key insights

- Setbacks can be delayed greenlights. A refusal, detour or failure often proves useful later, so ask what it is teaching rather than only what it costs.
- Keep a journal. McConaughey built this book from decades of notes, and writing things down let him look back and learn from what had worked and what had not.
- Saying no can change your direction. Turning down comfortable, well-paid work created space for the kind of opportunities he actually wanted.
- Knowing what you do not want is progress. Eliminating the wrong options can clarify your direction faster than chasing every possibility.
- Step away when you lose perspective. At several turning points, especially after sudden fame, time alone and far from routine helped him reset his priorities.

*Read it if you enjoy funny, candid life stories and want a fresh way to think about setbacks and turning points.*
