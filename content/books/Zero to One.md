*Notes on Startups, or How to Build the Future*

**Peter Thiel** · 2014 · Business
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Based on a course Peter Thiel taught at Stanford and written with Blake Masters, Zero to One argues that real progress comes from creating something new, going from zero to one, rather than copying what already works, going from one to n. Thiel believes the future will not improve by itself, so founders need a definite plan and the confidence to think for themselves.

His most provocative claim is that competition destroys profits and that great businesses aim for monopoly. He advises dominating a small market first and expanding from there, using proprietary technology, network effects, economies of scale and branding. He also discusses the power law in venture investing, the value of secrets others have missed, founding teams, and why distribution matters as much as product.

## Key insights

- Look for important truths that most people overlook or reject; durable companies are often built on such secrets.
- Competition erodes profits. Aim to be so much better in a small, specific market that you face little direct competition, then expand outward.
- Returns often follow a power law: a few successes outweigh everything else, so concentrate on the few bets that could matter most.
- Aim for technology that is about ten times better than existing options on something customers care about; marginal gains rarely create lasting advantage.
- Distribution is as important as product. Many good products fail because their creators never planned how to reach customers.

*Read it if you are building or investing in a company and want a contrarian framework for thinking about innovation and competition.*
