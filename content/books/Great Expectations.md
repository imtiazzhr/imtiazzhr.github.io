**Charles Dickens** · 1861 · Classic novel
<span class="book-shelf">Currently reading</span>

Pip is an orphan growing up on the Kent marshes, raised by his harsh older sister and her gentle husband, Joe, the village blacksmith. The story opens with a terrifying meeting with an escaped convict in a churchyard. Soon afterwards Pip is summoned to Satis House, where the wealthy, bitter Miss Havisham lives among stopped clocks, still dressed for the wedding that never took place. There he meets Estella, a proud and beautiful girl, and begins to feel ashamed of his home and his future at the forge.

When a lawyer announces that an unnamed benefactor will pay for him to become a gentleman in London, Pip's life changes. Dickens follows his ambitions and illusions, exploring class, snobbery, guilt, loyalty and what makes a person good.

## Key insights

- Social rank and good character are different things. The novel's moral centre is Joe, an uneducated blacksmith with no wish to rise in the world.
- Shame about where you come from can make you treat loyal people badly. Pip's snobbery hurts those who love him most.
- Money that arrives without effort or purpose can weaken judgement. Pip's expectations leave him idle, in debt and careless with the people around him.
- Miss Havisham shows how refusing to move on from an old wound can freeze a life and damage others drawn into it.
- Told by an older Pip looking back, the novel shows how easily young people misread their own motives and mistake status for happiness.

*Read it if you enjoy character-driven stories about ambition, class and growing up, told with humour and moral depth.*
