**John Green** · 2012 · Young adult novel
<span class="book-shelf">Want to read</span>

Sixteen-year-old Hazel Grace Lancaster has cancer that has spread to her lungs. An experimental drug has bought her time, but she carries an oxygen tank everywhere and has few illusions about her future. At a support group her mother insists she attend, she meets Augustus Waters, a charming seventeen-year-old who lost a leg to bone cancer.

The two bond over Hazel's favourite novel, which ends mid-sentence, and set out to learn from its reclusive author what happens to its characters, a quest that takes them to Amsterdam. Told in Hazel's dry, funny voice, the novel deals with first love, mortality, the fear of being forgotten and what makes a life meaningful, while mocking the clichés of the typical cancer story.

## Key insights

- A short life can still be a full one; the novel suggests meaning comes from love and attention, not from length or fame.
- Hazel fears she is a grenade that will hurt everyone close to her; the book questions whether keeping people at a distance really protects them.
- Augustus dreads being forgotten and wants a grand legacy; Hazel argues that being deeply loved by a few people may be enough.
- Illness is shown as tiring, unfair and often dull rather than ennobling; the characters resent being treated as symbols of bravery.
- The need for closure runs deep; Hazel's hunt for her favourite novel's missing ending is really about knowing her loved ones will be all right.

*Read it if you want a funny, heartfelt story about young love and mortality that does not offer easy comfort.*
