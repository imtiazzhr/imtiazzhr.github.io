*Master the Art of Business*

**Josh Kaufman** · 2010 · Business
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Josh Kaufman argues that an expensive business degree is not the only way to learn how businesses work. After reading widely instead of enrolling in business school, he distilled the most useful ideas into short, plain-English entries that anyone can study on their own.

The book is organised as a set of mental models covering value creation, marketing, sales, value delivery and finance, followed by sections on human psychology, productivity, working with others and understanding systems. His central model is simple: every business creates something people want, attracts attention to it, persuades people to buy, delivers what it promised and brings in enough money to continue. Weakness in any one of these can sink the whole enterprise. It reads as both an introduction and a reference.

## Key insights

- Test any business idea against five parts: value creation, marketing, sales, value delivery and finance. A serious flaw in one can undo strength in the others.
- There are only four ways to grow revenue: more customers, larger transactions, more frequent purchases or higher prices. Naming the lever makes growth plans concrete.
- Launch a minimum viable offer and improve it through fast iteration. Early paying customers teach you more than months of private planning.
- People buy end results, not features. Describe the outcome customers want, then remove barriers to buying, for example by offering a strong guarantee that reverses their risk.
- Follow Gall's Law: complex systems that work grow out of simple systems that worked. Start simple, prove it works, then add complexity gradually.

*Read it if you want a broad, practical grounding in how businesses work without the cost of business school.*
