*An Easy & Proven Way to Build Good Habits & Break Bad Ones*

**James Clear** · 2018 · Productivity
<span class="book-shelf">Currently reading</span>

James Clear argues that lasting change comes from small habits compounding over time rather than from dramatic goals or bursts of motivation. Getting slightly better each day can add up to large results, although progress often stays invisible for a while before it shows. He recommends focusing on systems rather than goals, and on identity rather than outcomes, since each habit acts as a vote for the kind of person you want to become.

The core of the book is a practical framework built on a four-step habit loop of cue, craving, response and reward. From it Clear derives four laws of behaviour change: make it obvious, attractive, easy and satisfying. Reversing each law helps break bad habits.

## Key insights

- Anchor a new habit to an existing one with habit stacking: after a current routine, immediately do the new behaviour you want.
- Design your environment so good cues are visible and bad ones are hidden; willpower matters less than what surrounds you.
- Use the two-minute rule: scale a new habit down to a version you can start in two minutes, then build from there.
- Think in identities, not outcomes. Ask what a healthy or disciplined person would do, and let small actions prove it to yourself.
- Never miss twice. Missing one day is normal, but a second miss starts a new pattern, so return quickly after lapses.

*Read it if you want a clear, practical system for building good habits and breaking bad ones that you can start using today.*
