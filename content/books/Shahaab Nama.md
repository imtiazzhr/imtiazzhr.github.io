*شہاب نامہ*

**Qudratullah Shahab** · 1987 · Memoir
<span class="book-shelf">Read</span>

Shahaab Nama is the Urdu autobiography of Qudratullah Shahab, a writer and senior civil servant whose career ran from British India into Pakistan's first decades. Born in Gilgit in 1917, he joined the Indian Civil Service in 1941, reportedly the first Muslim from Jammu and Kashmir to do so. After independence he served as principal secretary to Governor-General Ghulam Muhammad and to Presidents Iskander Mirza and Ayub Khan, and later as an ambassador and federal secretary.

Published in 1987, the year after his death, the long book mixes memories of childhood and family, an insider's view of power, and accounts of his spiritual experiences. It is among the most widely read Urdu books, though critics have questioned some of its claims as exaggerated.

## Key insights

- Proximity to power shows how often national decisions depend on a few personalities, their health, rivalries and ambitions rather than formal process.
- Shahab presents personal integrity as a daily choice in public service, especially when superiors or circumstances make compromise the easier path.
- Spiritual seeking and a demanding worldly career can run side by side; Shahab's inner life is as much a subject as his offices.
- Insider memoirs are valuable but selective. Read them alongside other sources, since authors naturally shape events to fit their own story.
- Shahab's account suggests that simple living and humility can help protect a person from the temptations that come with high office.

*Read it if you read Urdu and want an insider's view of Pakistan's early decades mixed with personal and spiritual reflection.*
