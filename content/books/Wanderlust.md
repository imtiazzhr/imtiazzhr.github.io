**Elisabeth Eaves** · 2011 · Travel memoir
<span class="book-shelf">Want to read</span>

Elisabeth Eaves, a Canadian-American journalist raised in Vancouver, recounts roughly fifteen years of restless travel that began in her student days. Her journeys take her to places including Cairo, Yemen, Pakistan, Papua New Guinea and Paris, and almost every stage is tied to a romance, from a sweetheart from her school days to lovers met on the road.

The memoir is candid about sex, loneliness and risk, and about the appetite for unfamiliar places and people that keeps pulling her onward. Its central tension is between the freedom of moving on and the wish for a lasting relationship and a settled home. Reviewers have noted that the book is as much about love affairs as about places, so it suits readers who value inner journeys over guidebook detail.

## Key insights

- Travel can feel like reinvention, but the memoir shows that the same habits and questions tend to follow you from place to place.
- Restlessness and commitment pull against each other; Eaves examines why distance and impermanence felt safer to her than staying put.
- Travelling alone as a woman brings real freedom and real risk; she writes frankly about both rather than romanticising the road.
- Wanting what you cannot have can quietly steer a life; recognising that pattern is the first step towards choosing differently.
- Home can be a person, a place or even a habit of moving on; the book shows how hard it is to know which one you want.

*Read it if you are drawn to candid travel memoirs in which the inner journey, and love, matter as much as the destinations.*
