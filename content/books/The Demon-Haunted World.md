*Science as a Candle in the Dark*

**Carl Sagan** · 1995 · Science
<span class="book-shelf">Currently reading</span>

Carl Sagan argues that science is our best tool for separating truth from wishful thinking, and that a society which loses the habit of sceptical inquiry becomes easy to mislead. He examines popular beliefs of the 1990s, including alien abductions, a supposed face on Mars, faith healing and recovered memories of abuse, and sets them beside the witch hunts of earlier centuries.

The book is not a sneer at believers. Sagan presents scientific thinking as a skill anyone can learn, combining openness to new ideas with rigorous testing. He offers a practical toolkit for spotting weak arguments and common logical fallacies. He also warns that a society which depends on science and technology, yet barely understands them, risks sliding into superstition and manipulation by the powerful.

## Key insights

- Use a baloney detection kit: seek independent confirmation, consider several explanations, quantify where possible and prefer the simplest explanation that fits the evidence.
- A claim that cannot be tested, like an invisible dragon that leaves no trace, is not worth much, however sincerely it is held.
- Balance openness with scepticism. Too much openness makes you gullible, while too much doubt blinds you to new ideas that turn out to be true.
- Arguments from authority carry little weight. Experts can be wrong, so a claim has to stand on its evidence rather than on who makes it.
- Science is trustworthy because it corrects itself. Admitting and fixing errors is built into the method, unlike belief systems that never change their answers.

*Read it if you want to sharpen your scepticism and learn practical tools for testing the claims you meet every day.*
