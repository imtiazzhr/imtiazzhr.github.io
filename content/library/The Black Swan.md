**Nassim Nicholas Taleb** · 2007

Taleb argues that history is driven by rare, unpredictable events with enormous impact, which we explain away only after they happen. Because we can't forecast them, we should build lives, businesses and portfolios that survive, or even benefit from, surprises.

## Key ideas

- We confuse the absence of evidence with evidence of absence.
- The narrative fallacy: we invent neat stories to explain randomness.
- Some domains are mild and predictable (Mediocristan); others are wild and dominated by extremes (Extremistan).
- Prefer robustness over forecasts.
